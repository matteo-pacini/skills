# Banned words, phrases, and structures

All of these are encoded in `scripts/lint.py`. The list is a set of defaults to override
deliberately, not a law; see "On the banned list" in SKILL.md. Fix a flag by rewriting
the sentence, not by swapping in a synonym.

## Words

Never use: delve, dive into, navigate (figurative), underscore, bolster, foster, harness,
leverage, unpack, unlock, shed light on, pave the way, streamline, elevate, empower,
pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changing, game-changer,
innovative, robust, comprehensive, seamless, intricate, vibrant, multifaceted, holistic,
testament, tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, ecosystem (figurative), journey
(figurative), crucial, vital, key (as adjective), myriad, plethora, meticulous, curated,
bespoke, actionable, granular.

Enthusiasm words: exciting, powerful, incredible, amazing. And no exclamation marks.

Latinate swaps: utilise → use, facilitate → help, demonstrate → show, commence → start,
purchase → buy, sufficient → enough, additional → more, numerous → many, obtain → get,
require → need, assist → help, implement → do/build, prior to → before, in order to → to.

## Phrases

- "In today's [fast-paced / rapidly evolving / digital] world"
- "It's important to note that" / "It's worth noting that" / "That said"
- "One of the most [important / significant / crucial]"
- "When it comes to" / "At its core" / "At the end of the day" / "The reality is"
- "This is where X comes in" / "Let's break it down" / "Let's dive in"
- "plays a crucial role in" / "cannot be overstated"
- "underscoring the importance of" / "highlighting the need for"
- "reflecting a broader trend toward" / "marking a significant shift in"
- "In conclusion" / "In summary" / "Ultimately" / "Overall" (as paragraph openers)
- Any sentence beginning "Whether you're X or Y"

## Structures

- Negative parallelism: "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y" /
  "Not only X, but also Y". State the claim directly.
- Triads: three adjectives, three benefits, three examples, whenever three is a rhythm
  rather than a fact. If there are two reasons, give two.
- The staccato fragment run: "No fluff. No filler. Just results."
- Tailing participial clauses that add significance rather than information:
  "...changing how teams work" / "...reshaping the industry".
- The rhetorical-question opener: "Ever wondered why...?" / "What if I told you".
- "As an X, you know that..." flattery openers.
- The seesaw: "While X, it's important to consider Y." Split into two claims.
- Topic-sentence-then-three-supports, repeated down the page.
- "**Bold term**: explanation sentence" lists.
- Opening a paragraph by announcing its contents; closing it by summarising them.
- Ending a paragraph or the piece on a flourish about significance.
